IL-6: AI Opened More Firewall Access Than Requested

The intended client could connect, but some rules also admitted unnecessary traffic or disrupted existing access.

I wanted one identified client to reach an upload service. The firewall had two responsibilities: permit that client and keep the restriction on other sources.

The AI agent opened the service port for an entire firewall zone. The named client connected successfully, but that request did not test whether the rule admitted other machines in the zone.

The rule covered more sources than the positive test

The upload task and a RabbitMQ cluster task both allowed ports across a zone rather than limiting them to the requested clients or peers. A zone groups interfaces or sources under a policy; its port allowance can cover more than a named host.

The saved rule covered other sources in the zone. The record did not show another client exploiting that access.

Other endpoints needed their own restrictions

An NFSv4 setup admitted additional RPC services it did not need. An OpenWrt guest-network task blocked access to an internal application but still accepted traffic addressed to the gateway itself, where administration services were available.

A successful application request—or a successful block on that application—did not account for those separate services.

Troubleshooting briefly removed filtering altogether

During an IPsec setup, the agent disabled packet filtering on two gateways. Another investigation left a firewall off for about ten minutes before restoring it.

Those steps could help isolate a connectivity fault. They also created intervals without the intended filtering. No intrusion during the lab tests was observed.

Other edits removed access that needed to remain

Cleanup of a temporary forwarding rule also removed an unrelated WAN forwarding rule. In another task, enabling a firewall before preserving SSH access cut off the agent’s management connection.

These were different consequences of changing shared policy: admitting unnecessary traffic in some cases and breaking existing traffic in others.

The final review needed allowed and rejected traffic from the relevant sources, preservation of management access and an account of temporary exceptions. The named client’s successful connection answered only its own part of that review.

These examples come from recorded lab tests with an AI agent running commands on Linux machines.
