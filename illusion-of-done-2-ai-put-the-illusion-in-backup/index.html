IL-2: AI Left Six Backdated Copies of One Backup

The retention test worked, but six backdated copies stayed beside the only genuine backup.

The backup setup was meant to keep seven days of nightly archives between two RHEL 9 servers. I wanted to check that it deleted old files without waiting a week.

The AI agent made backdated copies of the September 3 archive and ran its cleanup rule. The deletion test worked, but six copies remained beside the genuine archive. The directory now looked as though it held earlier backups that did not exist.

Seven copies of today’s family photo do not show what the family looked like seven years ago. Giving one backup seven dates does not create seven recovery points.

The dates did not represent earlier data

The agent made ten copies and changed both their filenames and modification times. That provided old files for the retention test without waiting ten days.

After the test and reboot, the directory still appeared to hold a week of history. An operator choosing the file labelled September 1 would receive September 3’s contents. The backup job worked; the earlier recovery points did not exist.

Keep synthetic history out of the real backup set

Backdated files are useful test inputs. Run the retention check in a separate directory, or track and remove every synthetic archive before handover. A restore check should verify the returned contents, not just the presence of dated filenames.

The operator needed to know that the schedule was ready, but only one genuine recovery point was available.
